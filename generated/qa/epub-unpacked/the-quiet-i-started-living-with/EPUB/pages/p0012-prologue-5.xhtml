This only refined my inner patterns.

There is a kind of comfort hidden inside mental noise.

Not because it feels good—but because it is predictable.

And predictable pain often feels safer than unfamiliar peace.

I did not realize I was choosing familiarity over experience.

Every day.

Without deciding.

Without resistance.

This is not a story about something that broke.

It is about something that slowly replaced everything else.

Presence replaced by interpretation.

Living replaced by replaying.

Experience replaced by explanation.

And the gap between them never felt like a gap.

It felt like thinking.

This book is not an attempt to define silence.

Silence does not need interpretation.

It is an attempt to observe what happens when a person becomes more fluent in their thoughts than in their own life.

When explanation becomes easier than experience.
