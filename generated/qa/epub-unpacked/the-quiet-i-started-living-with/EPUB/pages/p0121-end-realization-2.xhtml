But it was repetition mistaken for insight.

The mind has a quiet tendency:

what is revisited often starts to feel significant,

and what feels significant begins to feel true.

That is how repetition becomes identity—without ever becoming truth.

I thought I was remembering my life.

I was editing it, in real time.

Memory is never fixed.

It is rebuilt each time it is recalled.

And it is rebuilt from the present, not the past.

Fear reshapes it.

Regret reshapes it.

Expectation reshapes it.

So what I carried was never what happened.

Only my current interpretation of what happened.

And I called that “me.”

There was no decisive moment of change.

No collapse.

No clarity.

No revelation.
