But interpretation survives in the present.

It adjusts itself quietly—according to fear, regret, pride, and expectation.

Until eventually, you are no longer carrying the event itself.

You are carrying the version of it your mind refused to stop editing.

There was a night I remember clearly—not because it was important, but because it wasn’t.

I was sitting in my room, lights off, phone face down, replaying a conversation that had ended hours ago. Nothing had gone wrong in any visible sense. And yet my mind kept trying to “fix” what could no longer be changed.

That was when I first noticed something strange.

The silence around me was not silent at all.

It was filled.

Not with sound—but with commentary.

Questions without answers.

Replies I should have given.

Explanations no one had asked for.

I used to think silence was outside me.

An empty room.
