Without noticing, you begin spending more time inside your mind than inside your life.

At first, it looks harmless.

You replay a conversation from earlier in the day.

You recheck a tone, a pause, a word you shouldn’t have said.

You imagine alternative endings to things that already ended.

It feels ordinary. Almost responsible. Like understanding.

Everyone does it.

But the mind does not treat repetition neutrally.

It rewards it.

And what is rewarded is returned to.

Again.

And again.

Until what was once a passing thought becomes a familiar place.

And familiarity slowly changes its meaning.

It starts convincing you that repetition is clarity.

That revisiting something is the same as understanding it.

That was the illusion I never questioned.

I believed I was remembering my life.

In truth, I was reconstructing it—each time slightly differently.

A memory belongs to what happened.
