His approach is not philosophical declaration, but careful observation.

Not belief, but inquiry.

Not conclusion, but clarity emerging through attention.

The Human Fiction reflects this perspective — exploring how identity, meaning, and personal continuity are formed through narrative patterns that operate silently in the background of awareness.

In his view, most of what we call “self” is not discovered…

it is continuously written.

And most of what we call “life” is not directly interpreted…

it is continuously narrated.

This work is not meant to be taken as truth or rejected as fiction.

It is meant to be seen as structure.

And what is seen clearly…

no longer holds the same unconscious authority it once did.
