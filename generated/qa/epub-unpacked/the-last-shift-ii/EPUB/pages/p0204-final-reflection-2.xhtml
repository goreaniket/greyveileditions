You were never inside a “story” in the way you believed. You were inside a constant act of editing reality into something survivable, something narratable, something emotionally organized enough to not overwhelm perception.

But reality itself was never organized for you.

It never needed to be.

There is a moment—subtle, almost imperceptible—when awareness stops being a participant in thought and becomes its witness. Not elevated. Not superior. Just displaced from identification.

In that displacement, something unsettling appears:

Nothing is holding your thoughts together except attention.

And attention is not stable.

This is where the illusion of control begins to dissolve—not dramatically, but structurally. You realize you were never steering the content of mind. You were only moving within it, reacting to its momentum, calling it “choice” because randomness feels unbearable without a name.

Even pain was misread.

Not because it wasn’t real, but because it was immediately converted into narrative before it could remain as pure sensation. And narrative always adds distance. That distance becomes personality.
