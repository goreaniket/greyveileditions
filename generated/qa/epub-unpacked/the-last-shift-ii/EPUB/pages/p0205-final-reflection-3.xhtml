Healing, then, was never repair. It was subtraction.

The removal of unnecessary ownership from experience.

The slow disappearance of the belief that every internal event requires interpretation, resolution, or meaning-making.

What remains after that removal is not peace in the romantic sense. It is clarity without insulation. A directness that cannot be comfortably lived inside language.

You still think.

But thinking stops being an identity and becomes an event.

You still feel.

But feeling stops being a statement about self and becomes weather passing through perception.

The “self” does not die. It is simply no longer mistaken for the center.

And that is the dangerous part—not danger in harm, but danger in irreversibility. Once seen, it cannot be fully unseen. The mind may rebuild narratives, but something underneath remembers the structure was never fixed.

There is no final enlightenment here. No arrival. No completion.

Only a reduction in unnecessary distortion between what is happening and what is being said about it internally.
